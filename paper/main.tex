\begin{abstract}
Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) returns a reward, and a policy is then
optimized against it as if it were correct. Conformal inverse optimization
(CIO) offers a principled alternative for one-shot inverse problems: calibrate
a radius around the point estimate on held-out decisions, then optimize
robustly over the resulting set. We ask what survives the move to IRL. The
conformal step transfers untouched; the score and its downstream interpretation
require care under reward non-identifiability. First, CIO scores a demonstration
by an angle between reward vectors, which depends on coordinates the problem
never fixes: a feature reparameterization
that changes no reward and no demonstrator's policy still moves the calibrated
angular radius by $24^\circ$ on average at condition number $200$. Scoring instead
by the spread a reward difference induces over achievable policy values restores invariance and keeps
the score a linear program. Second, building on CIO's intersection guarantee
and inverse-set diameter assumption, we distinguish the events certified
by different distances to the cone of rewards explaining new behavior: the
nearest point gives intersection with that cone, the farthest point of the
normalized cone gives containment of the latent reward. The first admits a
general LP formulation, and at a $0.80$ target we measure the two events at $0.82$ and
$0.44$. We give the enlarged radius that
prices the relaxation, and the point past which the resulting robust program
has zero worst-case advantage over its reference. This threshold concerns our
ball-based prescription, not an impossibility for all decisions from behavior.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

IRL turns observed behavior into a reward estimate, and that estimate is then
optimized to pick a policy. Uncertainty is dropped exactly where it matters,
because two rewards that explain the demonstrations equally well can recommend
different actions. Conformal inverse optimization \citep{lin2024conformal} is
an appealing remedy for the analogous one-shot problem: observe a population of
decision makers, fit a point estimate on part of it, and on the rest record how
far that estimate sits from the objectives that would rationalize each held-out
decision. The conformal quantile of those distances defines an uncertainty set
with a finite-sample guarantee, and a robust optimization over that set replaces
the point prescription. The recipe is modular and looks portable. This paper
reports what happens when it is carried into IRL, component by component
(Table~\ref{tab:recipe}).

One component transfers for free. Once per-demonstrator scores exist, the
calibration arithmetic---%
rank them, take the $\lceil\gamma(N+1)\rceil$-th, invoke exchangeability---is
split conformal prediction exactly as in \citet{vovk2005algorithmic} and
\citet{lin2024conformal}; we inherit it wholesale. Sequential decision making
changes nothing about that argument. The work is in the two components on either
side of it: which distance gets calibrated, and what the resulting event is
good for.

\paragraph{The score is measured in the wrong space.} CIO scores a
demonstration by the angle between the point estimate and the nearest objective
that rationalizes it. In IRL that angle lives in coordinates the problem does
not fix. A reward vector can change without changing the difference between any
two policies' values: add a potential-shaping term, or alter the reward on a
state no policy reaches. The angle registers such a change; behavior cannot. An
uncertainty set built from angles can therefore hit its target rate while
much of its radius covers differences no demonstration could exhibit. This is
not hypothetical:
we apply an invertible feature reparameterization that leaves every scalar
reward and every demonstrator's policy pointwise unchanged, and the calibrated
angular radius still moves by $24^\circ$ on average (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
Two identical problems get two different uncertainty sets. The fix is not a
better estimator but a prior question: what does behavior actually distinguish?
Everything observable here factors through the achievable occupancy measures,
so we measure a reward difference $v$ by the spread of policy values it
produces, $\Dnorm{v}=\max_\mu
V_v(\mu)-\min_\mu V_v(\mu)$, a seminorm whose null space is exactly the
directions behavior cannot see.

\paragraph{Intersection is not containment.} This distinction is already
explicit in CIO's intersection guarantee; its Assumption~2 bounds the
Euclidean diameter of the inverse-feasible set and its Theorem~3 uses that
bound for decision quality \citep{lin2024conformal}. Here we express the
corresponding identification requirement in normalized occupancy-span geometry.
Split conformal certifies that \emph{some} reward in the calibrated set makes the new
demonstrator optimal; our route to reference-relative safety requires the
demonstrator's \emph{own} normalized reward to lie in the set. These events need
not be close in either inverse optimization or IRL. At
target $0.8$ we measure the first event at $0.82$ and the second at $0.44$, and
no amount of calibration closes that gap. The two events differ by which
distance to the cone is calibrated. Scoring the distance to the \emph{nearest} point---%
the quantity a linear program can compute---certifies intersection. Scoring the
distance to the \emph{farthest} point of the normalized cone certifies
containment for nonnull latent rewards without a width assumption, but is
generally a nonconvex maximization. Our
construction is the convex relaxation together with an explicit account of what
it costs: a factor of two from renormalizing the witness, plus a certified
bound $\eta$ on the cone's width, giving $R=\min\{2,2q_\gamma+\eta\}$. Using
only the universal bound $\eta=2$ makes this ball-based prescription degenerate.
A sharper certificate may follow from the known geometry itself or from
additional information; Appendix~\ref{app:exact-toy} supplies a small exact example.

What we report is diagnostic rather than a performance improvement: half of
CIO's pipeline moves to IRL untouched, two components must be rebuilt in
decision space, and the step from intersection to containment is a relaxation
whose price we state rather than hide. Regret
against point-estimate IRL is therefore not the yardstick, and
\S\ref{sec:experiments} reports where our construction does and does not lower
it.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

This work sits between two literatures. Inverse
optimization recovers objectives from observed decisions
\citep{ahuja2001inverse,chan2018inverse}, and CIO adds finite-sample
calibration to it \citep{lin2024conformal}; this is a different problem from
data-driven robust optimization, where the coefficients are observed or
predicted rather than latent
\citep{chenreddy2022datadriven,sun2023predictthencalibrate}. IRL has known
since \citet{ng2000algorithms} that behavior determines a reward only up to a
cone, and recent work characterizes those feasible sets and asks what extra
structure shrinks them---experts of known suboptimality under a generative
model \citep{poiani2024suboptimal}, several demonstrators of declared
suboptimality \citep{kim2026feasible}, a unified LP view of demonstrations and
feedback \citep{kim2024unified}, or an account of what behavior provably cannot
resolve \citep{skalse2023invariance} and what that costs a downstream decision
\citep{lazzati2025robustness}. That line
attaches no predictive coverage guarantee for a new demonstrator to its set.
CIO already treats set identification through intersection coverage and a
diameter assumption. Our adaptation uses the IRL occupancy geometry to obtain
an invariant LP score, a normalized containment bridge, and a precise
degeneracy threshold for the resulting reference-relative prescription.

For the geometry we borrow rather than reinvent. EPIC canonicalizes shaping away \citep{gleave2021epic}, DARD
restricts to realizable transitions \citep{wulfe2022dard}, and STARC generalizes
the canonicalize-then-normalize recipe, showing that any pseudometric with
worst-case-regret upper \emph{and} lower bounds is bilipschitz equivalent to a
STARC metric
\citep{skalse2024starc}. Our occupancy span plays the role of a
canonicalization--normalization pair in the same spirit, taken relative to the
known $P$, so we propose no new reward metric; what is new is using
this geometry as a conformal score over Bellman cones, and being explicit about
the identification assumption the resulting radius still needs. On the robust
side, tractability is classically recovered by rectangularity
\citep{iyengar2005robust,nilim2005robust,wiesemann2013robust}, and
\citet{gadot2024solving} show that non-rectangular, ball-shaped reward
ambiguity is tractable in occupancy space---the space we also work in, with the difference
that their ambiguity set is given rather than calibrated. Conformal methods
have appeared in RL and decision making for other purposes \citep{gupta2023cammarl,
pan2026uncertaintyaware,zhou2025calibrating}, but we are not aware of prior
work that calibrates an inverse-feasible reward set from a population of IRL
demonstrators.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setting}

A tabular MDP $M=(S,A,P,\rho_0,\beta,\phi)$ has known kernel $P$, initial
distribution $\rho_0$, discount $\beta\in[0,1)$, and features
$\phi:S\times A\to\mathbb R^d$ inducing $r_\theta(s,a)=\phi(s,a)^\top\theta$.
A population of demonstrators draws rewards
$\hat\theta_1,\dots,\hat\theta_N\sim\Lambda$ i.i.d.; demonstrator $k$ acts
optimally under $\hat\theta_k$, producing a deterministic policy $\pi_k$ that we
assume is observed exactly rather than estimated from finite trajectories. Let
$M(P)$ be the polytope of normalized discounted occupancy measures, $\Phi$ the
$(|S||A|)\times d$ feature matrix, and
$V_\theta(\mu):=\theta^\top\Phi^\top\mu/(1-\beta)$. The rewards that make an
observed policy optimal form the cone
\[
K(\pi):=\{\theta:Q^\pi_\theta(s,a)\le Q^\pi_\theta(s,\pi(s))\ \forall s,a\}.
\]
Substituting the Bellman resolvent removes the unknown value function and makes
$K(\pi)$ exactly $|S|(|A|-1)$ halfspaces in $\theta$ alone
(Proposition~\ref{prop:resolvent}), which is the sequential restatement of
\citeauthor{ng2000algorithms}'s feasible-reward characterization.

\begin{assumption}[Exchangeable calibration unit]
\label{ass:unit}
The pairs $(\hat\theta_k,\pi_k)$,
$k=1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{val}},N_{\mathrm{val}}{+}1$, are i.i.d.\ (hence
exchangeable), and $M$---in particular $P$---is shared and fixed across all
demonstrators and the deployment instance.
\end{assumption}

Each demonstrator therefore plays the role of one (decision, context) pair in
CIO's setting; per-trajectory exchangeability within a single demonstrator
would instead be broken by within-trajectory Markov dependence. The pipeline is
Table~\ref{tab:recipe} read top to bottom: split the population, fit
$\bar\theta$ on the training split with any IRL method, calibrate on the
rest---write $N_{\mathrm{val}}$ for the size of that split---convert
intersection into containment, and solve a robust MDP. The
inherited CIO baseline uses the spherical cap
$C(\bar\theta,\alpha)=\{\theta:\|\theta\|_2\le1,\
\theta^\top\bar\theta\ge\cos\alpha\}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:angular}); that
specific choice, not the calibrate-then-robustify idea, is what reward
non-identifiability damages.

\section{Calibrating in decision space}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{A distance that behavior can see}
\label{sec:seminorm}

For $v\in\mathbb R^d$, define
\[
\Dnorm{v}:=\max_{\mu\in M(P)}V_v(\mu)-\min_{\mu\in M(P)}V_v(\mu)
= V^*_v(\rho_0)+V^*_{-v}(\rho_0)
= h_{D_F}(v),
\]
the spread of policy values that $v$ produces, i.e.\ the support function
$h_{D_F}$ of the feature difference body
$D_F:=\Phi^\top(M(P)-M(P))/(1-\beta)$, a form we return to in
\S\ref{sec:policy}. The right-hand form is two value iterations; no linear
program is needed for the seminorm itself.

\begin{proposition}[Properties of $\Dnorm{\cdot}$]
\label{prop:da-seminorm}
$\Dnorm{\cdot}$ is a seminorm on $\mathbb R^d$, and $\Dnorm{v}=0$ exactly when
$v\in\mathcal N:=\{a: a^\top\Phi^\top(\mu-\nu)=0\ \forall\mu,\nu\in M(P)\}$, the
subspace of directions that add the same constant to every policy's value.
$\mathcal N$ contains the potential-shaping subspace and can be strictly larger.
For any $\theta,\theta'$, with $\mu^*_\theta$ optimal for $\theta$ and
$\mu_{\theta'}$ optimal for $\theta'$,
\[
\mathrm{Reg}_\theta(\pi_{\theta'}):=V_\theta(\mu^*_\theta)-V_\theta(\mu_{\theta'})
\;\le\;\Dnorm{\theta-\theta'}.
\]
Finally $\Dnorm{\cdot}$ is exactly invariant under $\phi\mapsto A\phi$,
$\theta\mapsto A^{-\top}\theta$ for any invertible $A$.
\end{proposition}

The regret constant is $1$, with no Cauchy--Schwarz slack and no feature-norm
Lipschitz factor, because the seminorm is defined as the value spread it has to
bound. No seminorm that bounds regret can be smaller.

\begin{proposition}[Minimality]
\label{prop:minimal}
Let $N$ be any seminorm on $\mathbb R^d$ with
$\mathrm{Reg}_\theta(\pi_{\theta'})\le N(\theta-\theta')$ for all
$\theta,\theta'$. Then $N(v)\ge\Dnorm{v}$ for every $v$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $v$ and $\delta>0$, and take $\theta=v$, $\theta'=-\delta v$. Any policy
optimal for $\theta'$ maximizes $V_{-\delta v}$ and so minimizes $V_v$, while
$\mu^*_\theta$ maximizes $V_v$; hence
$\mathrm{Reg}_\theta(\pi_{\theta'})=\max_\mu V_v-\min_\mu V_v=\Dnorm{v}$. The
hypothesis with $\theta-\theta'=(1+\delta)v$ and homogeneity of $N$ give
$\Dnorm{v}\le N((1+\delta)v)=(1+\delta)N(v)$. Let $\delta\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

So $\Dnorm{\cdot}$ is the pointwise smallest seminorm that bounds worst-case
regret: any valid alternative is at least as large, and a strictly larger one
certifies a strictly weaker statement at the same radius. The restriction to
$\delta>0$ is not cosmetic---at $\delta=0$ every policy is optimal for
$\theta'=0$ and the construction says nothing. Since shaping directions lie in $\mathcal N$, replacing $\bar\theta$ by
$\bar\theta+c$ for $c\in\mathcal N$ leaves both the score and the calibrated set
unchanged; the angular score, computed from $\bar\theta$'s normalized
coordinates, has no such property. Optimal behavior cannot identify positive
reward scale either, and we remove that symmetry without building quotient
coordinates: for any behaviorally nonnull reward $\theta$ we set
$\theta^\circ:=\theta/\Dnorm{\theta}$, and compare rewards by
$\Dnorm{\theta^\circ-\theta'^\circ}$, a pseudometric with range $[0,2]$ that
vanishes exactly when $\theta'=c\theta+a$ for some $c>0$, $a\in\mathcal N$, and
that inherits the normalized regret bound
(Proposition~\ref{prop:projective}).

\subsection{Score, quantile, and what they certify}
\label{sec:score}

Write $\bar\theta^\circ=\bar\theta/\Dnorm{\bar\theta}$ for the \emph{unit-span
center} and define the \emph{span score} $s_k$ and \emph{span ball} $B_q$,
\[
s_k:=\min_{\theta\in K(\pi_k)}\Dnorm{\theta-\bar\theta^\circ},
\qquad
B_q:=\{\theta:\Dnorm{\theta-\bar\theta^\circ}\le q\}.
\]
Throughout, \emph{coverage} names only the property $\Pr[E]\ge\gamma$ for a
stated event $E$. The two events at issue are \emph{intersection},
$K(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})\cap B\ne\emptyset$, and \emph{containment},
$\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}^\circ\in B$. Split conformal delivers coverage of
whichever event the score's sublevel set defines, and nothing more.

Because $\Dnorm{\cdot}$ has a linear-program epigraph and $K(\pi_k)$ is
polyhedral, $s_k$ is a single LP in $d+2|S|$ variables, with no second-order
cone anywhere; this is what would be lost by intersecting the cone with a
Euclidean ball. Since $0\in K(\pi_k)$ and $\Dnorm{\bar\theta^\circ}=1$, we have
$s_k\in[0,1]$, and both the score and its quantile are dimensionless and
unchanged under $\bar\theta\mapsto c\bar\theta+a$ for $c>0$, $a\in\mathcal N$.

\begin{proposition}[Intersection coverage]
\label{prop:da-coverage}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:unit}, let $q_\gamma$ be the $\tau$-th smallest $s_k$
among $N_{\mathrm{val}}$ calibration demonstrators, with
$\tau=\lceil\gamma(N_{\mathrm{val}}+1)\rceil$ and the sentinel $+\infty$ if
$\tau=N_{\mathrm{val}}+1$. For a fresh i.i.d.\ demonstrator,
$s_{\mathrm{new}}\le q_\gamma$ holds if and only if
$K(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})\cap B_{q_\gamma}\ne\emptyset$, and
$\Pr[K(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})\cap B_{q_\gamma}\ne\emptyset]\ge\gamma$.
\end{proposition}

The probability is the standard split-conformal rank argument
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic} applied verbatim. What belongs to this construction
is the equivalence, immediate from $s_k$ being a minimum: coverage of the score
\emph{is} intersection coverage of the ball, with no lossy conversion in
between. The contrast is the obvious repair of the angular cap, which
calibrates the value gap under $\bar\theta$ and converts it to a Euclidean ball
by inverting a Lipschitz bound (Appendix~\ref{app:angular}). That bound
licenses gap $\le$ const $\times$ distance and never the reverse, so its radius
certifies neither event---a measured containment rate of $0.004$ in
Figure~\ref{fig:metric}(b).

\subsection{From intersection to containment}
\label{sec:stage2}

$K(\pi)$ is not an equivalence class. Even after removing scale and null
directions it can contain rewards with genuinely different policy orderings, so
touching the cone says little about where the demonstrator's reward is. Write
$F^\circ(\pi):=\{\theta\in K(\pi):\Dnorm{\theta}=1\}$ for the normalized cone,
understood modulo the null directions.

Which event the quantile certifies depends only on which distance to
$F^\circ(\pi)$ is scored. The latent reward lies in
$F^\circ(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})$, so scoring the \emph{largest} distance,
\[
s^{\max}(\pi):=\max_{u\in F^\circ(\pi)}\Dnorm{u-\bar\theta^\circ},
\]
gives $\Dnorm{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}^\circ-\bar\theta^\circ}\le
s^{\max}(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})$ pointwise, and the same split-conformal argument
then certifies containment directly, under Assumption~\ref{ass:unit} and
nonnull latent rewards, without a cone-width certificate.
This is the score we want. It is also the maximization of a convex function
over a generally nonconvex set. We do not solve the general problem;
Appendix~\ref{app:exact-toy} solves a special case exactly. Our $s_k$ scores the
\emph{smallest} distance instead: a linear program, but it bounds the wrong
side. One assumption converts one into the other.

\begin{assumption}[Certified cone width]
\label{ass:cone-width}
There is a known $\eta\in[0,2]$ with
$\sup_{u,v\in F^\circ(\pi)}\Dnorm{u-v}\le\eta$ almost surely for the deployment
population, and each latent reward is behaviorally nonnull.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:cone-width}, $s^{\max}(\pi)\le2s(\pi)+\eta$
pointwise: when $s(\pi)<1$ the near witness renormalizes at a cost of
$2s(\pi)$ and the cone width covers the rest, and when $s(\pi)=1$ the bound
holds trivially because $s^{\max}\le2$. Since $t\mapsto2t+\eta$ is increasing
it commutes with order statistics, so the pointwise inequality passes straight
to the quantile, $q^{\max}_\gamma\le2q_\gamma+\eta=:R$, which is what
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} uses. Read this way,
Assumption~\ref{ass:cone-width} is additional information needed by this
LP-to-containment bound, not by exact maximum-distance calibration.
The factor $2$ renormalizes the witness, and $\eta$ stands in for what an exact
$s^{\max}$ would have measured. A small $\eta$ can be certified from a specific
known MDP, as in Appendix~\ref{app:exact-toy}. Side information, multiple
environments, or active comparisons can instead restrict the feasible set;
that route requires reformulating the score and certificate for the restricted
set. Without a sharper certificate we use the universal bound $\eta=2$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-stage containment and safe prescription]
\label{thm:containment}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unit}--\ref{ass:cone-width}, let $q_\gamma$ be the
Stage-I quantile and set $R:=\min\{2,\,2q_\gamma+\eta\}$. Then
$\Pr[\Dnorm{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}^\circ-\bar\theta^\circ}\le R]\ge\gamma$.
Let $\mu_R\in\arg\max_{\mu\in M(P)}\min_{\theta\in B_R}\big[V_\theta(\mu)
-V_\theta(\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})\big]$ for a fixed reference
$\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}\in M(P)$; then
\[
\Pr\!\left[V_{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}}(\mu_R)
\ge V_{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}}(\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})\right]\ge\gamma .
\]
\end{theorem}

The argument is short. On the Stage-I event some
$\tilde\theta\in K(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})$ is within $q_\gamma$ of the unit-span
center, so if $q_\gamma<1$ the reverse triangle inequality makes $\tilde\theta$
nonnull; normalizing it moves it by at most another $q_\gamma$, which is where
the factor $2$ comes from, and Assumption~\ref{ass:cone-width} then covers the
remaining distance to the latent reward inside the same cone. Minimizing over
$F^\circ(\pi)$ instead of $K(\pi)$ would remove the factor $2$, giving
$R=\min\{2,q_\gamma+\eta\}$; maximizing rather than minimizing over
$F^\circ(\pi)$ removes $\eta$ as well. Both are generally nonconvex, and both are what
keeping Stage I a linear program costs.

\subsection{The deployed policy}
\label{sec:policy}

$B_R$ is unbounded---it contains the whole affine subspace
$\bar\theta^\circ+\mathcal N$---so worst-case \emph{absolute} value over it is
unbounded below in general. The reading we take is that absolute value is the
wrong criterion: a
direction with no behavioral content can be scaled without bound, and only a
comparison between two occupancy measures is insensitive to it. We therefore
optimize worst-case value relative to a reference $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$. With
$x:=\Phi^\top(\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})/(1-\beta)$ and $D_F$ the feature
difference body $\Phi^\top(M(P)-M(P))/(1-\beta)$,
\[
\min_{\theta\in B_R}\big[V_\theta(\mu)-V_\theta(\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})\big]
=\big[V_{\bar\theta^\circ}(\mu)-V_{\bar\theta^\circ}(\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})\big]
-R\,\gamma_{D_F}(x),
\]
where $\gamma_{D_F}$ is the Minkowski gauge of $D_F$, the polar dual of
$\Dnorm{\cdot}$. Since $\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ ranges over $M(P)-M(P)$ by
construction, the penalty is automatically normalized to $[0,1]$, $R$ reads
directly in regret units, and no sign condition can arise. The gauge has an LP
epigraph, so the whole robust problem is one linear program.

\begin{proposition}[Degeneracy threshold]
\label{prop:degeneracy}
For the unit-span center, if $R\ge1$ the optimal relative robust value is zero,
attained by $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$; if $R>1$ every optimizer is feature-equivalent
to the reference. A nontrivial safe improvement therefore requires
$2q_\gamma+\eta<1$.
\end{proposition}

This is a necessary condition for strictly positive worst-case advantage for
this ball and objective, not a sufficient condition for improvement and not an
information-theoretic impossibility for other uncertainty sets or prescriptions.

Vacuity is therefore auditable: the implementation reports $q_\gamma$, $\eta$,
$R$, and whether $R<1$. One modelling choice remains. Centering at
$\bar\theta$'s own optimal occupancy is degenerate, since the reference is then
optimal for every $R$; all experiments instead center at the calibration
population's mean occupancy, which asks whether a policy beats what a random
draw from the population achieves. Since
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} guarantees dominance only against whichever
reference is chosen, a sensitivity analysis over that choice is required before
deployment.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The original experiments E1--E3 are deliberately narrow, each testing one link in the
calibrate--identify--prescribe chain: whether the calibrated radius survives an
intervention that changes nothing observable (E1), whether intersection
coverage implies containment (E2), and how strong an external certificate
Stage II would need (E3). Each isolates one mechanism rather than benchmarking
end-to-end regret. The separately labelled post-acceptance diagnostic in
Appendix~\ref{app:exact-toy} exercises a nondegenerate certificate and a
set-shape control; it does not change the E1--E3 results.

E2 and E3 use random tabular MDPs, a Voronoi-feature gridworld, and Objectworld
\citep{levine2011nonlinear}, each with discount $\beta=0.9$, $|S|\le36$, and
$d\le4$. We fit MaxEnt IRL \citep{ziebart2008maximum} and Bayesian IRL
\citep{ramachandran2007bayesian} by Metropolis--Hastings on 20 training demonstrators, calibrate on
20 at target level $\gamma=0.8$, and evaluate on 20 fresh demonstrators, for 10
environment seeds: 60 runs and
1{,}200 reward evaluations (600 distinct test draws, shared across estimators).
Draws within a run share a fitted center and radius, so
intervals are 95\% Student-$t$ intervals over the 30 environment--seed blocks
after averaging the two estimators within each block. Computation used a
20-core laptop, no GPU, and no commercial solver. An exact-fit suboptimality LP
baseline is not reported: it returned a span-zero center in one of 30 fits, for
which unit-span normalization is undefined, and we record that as an abstention
rather than perturbing the reward by an arbitrary epsilon.

computed from values rather than from coordinates---and sits lower still
because it needs no LP; \S\ref{sec:score} and Appendix~\ref{app:angular} show
that its converted radius nonetheless certifies neither event, so invariance
alone is not sufficient. Shading spans mean to worst over 10 seeds.
\textbf{(b)} At $\gamma=0.8$ every construction attains score-event coverage
at target: intersection for angular and span, value-gap coverage for the other.
Containment at the same Stage-I radius falls short. The right-hand bars use

The value-gap conversion certifies neither set event. Error bars are 95\% $t$ intervals over 30 environment--seed
blocks.}
\label{fig:metric}

\paragraph{E1: an intervention that changes nothing.} For each
$\kappa\in\{1,2,5,10,50,200\}$ and each of 10 seeds we draw a random MDP
($|S|=6$, $|A|=3$, $d=4$), a center $\bar\theta$, and 16 calibration
demonstrators, together with a random symmetric positive-definite $A$ of
condition number $\kappa$, and calibrate before and after
$\phi\mapsto A\phi$, $\theta\mapsto A^{-\top}\theta$; here the span score is
taken around $\bar\theta$ itself, which Proposition~\ref{prop:da-seminorm}
makes invariant as well. Coordinates change;
every scalar reward $\phi(s,a)^\top\theta$ does not (maximum numerical
discrepancy $2.1\times10^{-14}$), so a behaviorally meaningful radius should not
move. At $\kappa=200$ the angular radius changes by $0.424$ radians
($24.3^\circ$) on average and $1.071$ radians in the worst seed, while the span
radius changes by at most $2.0\times10^{-8}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:metric}a). This is a falsification test rather than a
favorable data distribution: the compared problems are the same problem.

\paragraph{E2: intersection holds, containment does not.} At
$\gamma=0.8$, pooled empirical intersection rates are $0.812\pm0.042$,
$0.838\pm0.046$, and $0.819\pm0.036$ for the angular, value-gap, and span
scores; the first and third are exactly inverse-set intersection, the second
only score coverage. Containment at the same Stage-I radius is only
$0.368\pm0.095$ for the angular cap and $0.438\pm0.081$ for the span ball,
while the value-gap-to-Euclidean conversion contains $0.004\pm0.005$
(Figure~\ref{fig:metric}b). Conformal ranking behaves exactly as the theory
says; containment does not follow from $q_\gamma$ alone. The separation is a property of which distance is scored, not
of the calibration: the same rank argument applied to $s^{\max}$ would place the
second bar at the target as well.

\paragraph{E3: how much identification is missing?} The calibrated $q_\gamma$
has pooled mean $0.316$, median $0.289$, and range $[0.019,0.871]$, so the
observable budget $b:=\max\{1-2q_\gamma,0\}$ is usually positive but small. A
certificate must satisfy $\eta<b$ to meet the necessary condition $R<1$
for strictly positive worst-case advantage: with $\eta=0$, $0.2$, and $0.5$,
respectively $85.0\%$, $76.7\%$, and $36.7\%$ of runs meet that condition,
without thereby establishing improvement. The environment matters (at
$\eta=0.2$, $95\%$ for random MDPs, $75\%$ for Objectworld, $60\%$ for
gridworld). The universal certificate $\eta=2$ gives $R=2$ everywhere, with
containment at radius $R$ equal to $1.000$, reference dominance $1.000$, and nontriviality
$0.000$---a consistency check of the degenerate case, where the implementation
explicitly returns the reference, not evidence of a useful safety improvement. The full
frontier is
in Figure~\ref{fig:identification-frontier}.

\paragraph{What these experiments do not show.} Using the Stage-I radius
directly can lower or raise true-reward regret; in the balanced six-cell audit
it lowers mean regret in two cells of six and raises it in four
(Table~\ref{tab:regret}). That policy is not covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}, which uses $R$, and our relative objective
guarantees dominance over the reference rather than over the point-estimate
policy. We do not report the scale, shift, and RLHF relabeling probes here:
they are exploratory and outside the claims of Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Metric choice is real and measurable (E1), but a conformal intersection radius
alone does not certify containment of the latent reward (E2).
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} prices that gap and E3 makes the price
observable: strictly positive worst-case advantage under this construction
requires a certified cone width below the run-specific budget $1-2q_\gamma$.
The universal width $2$ proves containment
but pushes the robust solution back to the reference: safe, but with no
decision content. This diagnoses the chosen radius bound and ball-based
prescription; it does not separate intrinsic non-identifiability from
conservatism introduced by the bound or the set shape. The control in
Appendix~\ref{app:exact-toy} illustrates that distinction. Two routes to a
more informative construction are as follows.

The algorithmic route is to compute $s^{\max}$ itself, which would need no
$\eta$ at all. Since $\Dnorm{v}$ is a maximum of finitely many linear
functionals of $v$---one per pair of vertices of $M(P)$---its unit ball is
polyhedral, so $F^\circ(\pi)$ is the intersection of a polyhedral cone with that
boundary, a union of finitely many polyhedral pieces, and the maximum of a
convex function over such a union is attained at a vertex of one of them. For
small $d$ this is enumerable; the complexity in general, and whether a useful
relaxation sits between the LP and the exact program, we leave open.
Appendix~\ref{app:exact-toy} implements an exactly enumerable case. The data
route is to certify a small $\eta$: repeated observations of one demonstrator
under several dynamics, active comparisons, or externally known restrictions on
the reward. The two routes are complementary---one shrinks what must be
assumed, the other strengthens what can be assumed---and either moves the same
frontier in Figure~\ref{fig:identification-frontier}.

Our exchangeability unit is demanding---a demonstrator with a fully observed
exact optimal policy in a shared known tabular MDP---and finite trajectories,
approximate optimality, subgroup-conditional coverage, and MDP shift each need separate
analysis. Covariate-shift weighting \citep{tibshirani2019conformal} may address
the statistical part of transfer but not the identifiability conditions studied
by \citet{schlaginhaufen2024towards}.

The closest setting we have not tested is preference-based reward modelling
\citep{christiano2017deep,rafailov2023direct}, where the same two questions
recur with a different calibration unit: one annotator, one calibration point.
A Bradley--Terry reward model is identified only up to the transformations that
leave every modelled comparison unchanged, so the rewards consistent with an
annotator's preferences again form a set rather than a point, and the
distinction this paper is about---whether a calibrated radius merely meets that
set or contains the annotator's own reward---applies unchanged. Two things do
change. The comparison set is a polyhedral cone only when the comparisons are
noiseless and the model is linear in features, so $K(\pi)$ must be replaced by
whatever the preference likelihood makes feasible; and annotators are not
exchangeable in the way Assumption~\ref{ass:unit} needs when they are recruited
in batches or disagree systematically. We raise this because a reward model
trained on preferences is then optimized against by a policy, which is
structurally the pipeline we have been auditing, not because our experiments
say anything about it. The pattern
still generalizes past IRL: whenever an inverse problem is only partially
identified, a calibrate-then-prescribe pipeline must answer three questions
separately---in which geometry the score is measured, how the quantile is
computed, and which event the calibrated set certifies about the latent
parameter---and the conformal rank argument supplies the coverage step once
the score and event are specified. CIO already states an intersection event
and an inverse-set diameter assumption. Here their consequences are made
explicit in IRL's normalized occupancy geometry; thin inverse sets are not
assumed to be an automatic property of one-shot problems.

\section{Deferred constructions}
\label{app:angular}

\begin{proposition}[Resolvent-based score reduction]
\label{prop:resolvent}
Fix $\pi$; let $P_\pi(s,s')=P(s,\pi(s),s')$, let $\Phi_\pi$ be the feature
matrix restricted to $\pi$'s actions, and let $W_\pi:=(I-\beta
P_\pi)^{-1}\Phi_\pi$. Then $V^\pi_\theta=W_\pi\theta$ and
$Q^\pi_\theta(s,a)=\Psi_\pi(s,a)^\top\theta$ with
$\Psi_\pi(s,a):=\phi(s,a)+\beta P(s,a,\cdot)W_\pi$, both linear in $\theta$, so
\[
K(\pi)=\{\theta:\
\Psi_\pi(s,a)^\top\theta\le\Psi_\pi(s,\pi(s))^\top\theta\ \forall s,a\}
\]
exactly. The angular baseline additionally uses
$\hat\Theta_2(\pi)=K(\pi)\cap\{\theta:\|\theta\|_2\le1\}$. Both have $d$
decision variables instead of $d+|S|$ and $|S|(|A|-1)$ nontrivial linear
inequalities, after one resolvent solve per distinct policy.
\end{proposition}

We verify this against a joint-$(\theta,V)$ reference to solver precision, at
$2$--$5.5\times$ the speed for $|S|$ from 20 to 100, and update it across
demonstrators by Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury in $O(k|S|^2+k^3)$ when policies
differ at $k$ states.

\begin{proposition}[Projective span geometry]
\label{prop:projective}
$d_\circ(\theta,\theta'):=\Dnorm{\theta^\circ-\theta'^\circ}$ is a pseudometric
with range $[0,2]$, invariant to independent positive rescaling, addition of
elements of $\mathcal N$, and invertible feature reparameterization. For
nonnull rewards $d_\circ(\theta,\theta')=0$ iff $\theta'=c\theta+a$ for some
$c>0$ and $a\in\mathcal N$, and
$\mathrm{Reg}_{\theta}(\pi_{\theta'})/\Dnorm{\theta}\le d_\circ(\theta,\theta')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Concavity under reward-only ambiguity; failure with dynamics ambiguity]
\label{thm:convexity}
\textbf{(a)} For \emph{any} nonempty compact $C\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, if $P$ is
fixed and known then $\max_{\mu\in M(P)}\min_{\theta\in C}\theta^\top\Phi^\top\mu$
is a concave maximization over the fixed polytope $M(P)$, hence a convex outer
problem whenever $C$ admits a tractable linear-minimization oracle.
\textbf{(b)} The guarantee need not survive when ambiguity couples reward and
transition: there is a 2-state, 2-action MDP and a convex one-dimensional
non-product ambiguity set over $(\theta,P)$ whose worst-case value function is
not concave.
\end{theorem}

Part (a) is why the robust step is routine once written in occupancy space, and
covers the fixed-kernel regime for an arbitrary compact set. Part (b) is
existential (Appendix~\ref{app:proof-convexity}); rectangular and other
structured cases remain tractable, and failure of concavity is not a hardness
proof. \citet{xu2026learning} instead use $d$-rectangular sets whose reward and
transition components vary together across data sources. Part~(a) does not
cover that setting, since fixing $P$ removes exactly the coupling; what it does
cover is the reward-only restriction of such a set, which is one instance of
the compact $C$.

\paragraph{The inherited angular baseline.} For this baseline only, we replace
every nonzero point estimate by its Euclidean normalization,
$\|\bar\theta\|_2=1$. With the score
$c_k=\max_{\theta\in\hat\Theta_2(\pi_k)}\theta^\top\bar\theta$ and
$\alpha_\gamma:=\arccos(q_\gamma)$, split-conformal calibration on $c_k$ is
CIO's exchangeability argument transplanted verbatim
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic}, giving
$\mathbb P(\hat\Theta_{2,\mathrm{new}}\cap
C(\bar\theta,\alpha_\gamma)\ne\emptyset)\ge\gamma$; we omit the routine
restatement. This is intersection nonemptiness, not containment. Because the
zero reward is Bellman-feasible for every policy, any cap with $\alpha\ge\pi/2$
makes the intersection event automatic, so intersection coverage alone never
establishes reward identification. Under the sign condition $a(x)\ge-\|x\|_2\cos\alpha$
(writing $x=a(x)\bar\theta+x_\perp$), the support function of the cap reduces to
$a(x)\cos\alpha-b(x)\sin\alpha$ with $b(x)=\|x_\perp\|_2$, so the robust policy
solves the point estimate with a fixed-weight $\ell_2$ penalty
$\tan\alpha_\gamma\cdot b(\Phi^\top\mu)$, set by the conformal quantile rather
than tuned.

\paragraph{The value-gap repair and why it is incomplete.} Replacing the
angular score by the suboptimality gap under $\bar\theta$,
$c_k:=V_{\bar\theta}(\mu^*_{\bar\theta})-V_{\bar\theta}(\mu_{\pi_k})$, removes
shaping contamination for free: shaping directions earn a constant on every
$\mu\in M(P)$ (an immediate consequence of the flow-conservation constraints
defining $M(P)$), so adding one to $\bar\theta$ leaves every $c_k$ unchanged.
But converting this score to a Euclidean ball of radius
$\rho:=q_\gamma/\bar\nu$ needs a Lipschitz constant
$\bar\nu:=\|\phi\|_{2,\infty}/(1-\beta)$, and the bound licenses gap $\le$
const $\times$ distance and never the reverse, so $q_\gamma$ alone certifies
nothing about the set (measured containment $0.004$,
Figure~\ref{fig:metric}b). Its robust program is at least
unconditional---$\min_{\|\theta-\bar\theta\|_2\le\rho}\theta^\top
x=\bar\theta^\top x-\rho\|x\|_2$ by Cauchy--Schwarz---but an unconditional
penalty on an uncertified radius is not a certificate.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Every identity and inequality stated in the main text is also checked
numerically, independently of the library routines it describes, by
\texttt{paper/verify\_formulas.py}: the two-solve form of $\Dnorm{\cdot}$
against brute-force enumeration of $M(P)$'s vertices, the seminorm axioms, the
regret bound, reparametrization invariance, the zero set $\mathcal N$, and the
gauge form of the deployed robust objective.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:resolvent}}
\label{app:proof-resolvent}

The value function $V^\pi_\theta$ solves the linear Bellman equation
$V^\pi_\theta = \Phi_\pi\theta + \beta P_\pi V^\pi_\theta$. Since
$\beta\in[0,1)$ and $P_\pi$ is stochastic, $\rho(\beta P_\pi)\le\beta<1$, so
$I-\beta P_\pi$ is invertible via a Neumann series and
$V^\pi_\theta = (I-\beta P_\pi)^{-1}\Phi_\pi\theta = W_\pi\theta$. Substituting
into the Bellman equation for $Q^\pi_\theta$,
\begin{align*}
Q^\pi_\theta(s,a) &= \phi(s,a)^\top\theta + \beta P(s,a,\cdot) V^\pi_\theta
= \phi(s,a)^\top\theta + \beta P(s,a,\cdot) W_\pi\theta
= \Psi_\pi(s,a)^\top\theta,
\end{align*}
which is linear in $\theta$. By policy improvement, $\pi$ is Bellman-optimal
for $\theta$ iff $Q^\pi_\theta(s,a)\le Q^\pi_\theta(s,\pi(s))$ for all $s,a$;
substituting the linear expression yields the stated cone with no relaxation,
since the substitution is an equality. The angular calibration score
$c_k=\max_{\theta\in\hat\Theta_2(\pi_k)}\theta^\top\bar\theta$ is then a linear
objective over $|S||A|$ linear inequalities plus a unit-ball constraint in
$\theta\in\mathbb R^d$ alone: an SOCP of size $d$, independent of $|S|$ once
$\Psi_{\pi_k}$ is precomputed. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:convexity}}
\label{app:proof-convexity}

\textbf{(a)} Define $g_C(x) := \min_{\theta\in C}\theta^\top x$, a pointwise
infimum of the affine family $\{x\mapsto\theta^\top x : \theta\in C\}$ and
hence concave for any nonempty $C$, convex or not. Composing with the linear
map $\mu\mapsto\Phi^\top\mu$ preserves concavity, so
$\mu \mapsto \min_{\theta\in C}\theta^\top\Phi^\top\mu$ is concave in $\mu$.
Since $M(P)$ is a fixed polytope not depending on $\theta$, the ambiguity set
never touches the feasible region, only the objective, and
$\max_{\mu\in M(P)} g_C(\Phi^\top\mu)$ is a concave maximization over a fixed
compact convex set. This establishes the convex geometry; efficient solution
also requires an efficient representation or linear-minimization oracle for
$C$. No Bellman recursion or rectangularity assumption enters.

\textbf{(b)} Let state $s_0$ be active and $s_1$ absorbing with zero reward.
At $s_0$, action $a_0$ earns zero and moves to $s_1$; action $a_1$ earns
$1+t$ and returns to $s_0$ with probability $1-t$ (otherwise moving to
$s_1$), where the same ambiguity parameter $t\in[0,1]$ couples reward and
dynamics. Both actions at $s_1$ self-loop with zero reward. Starting from
$s_0$, let a stationary policy choose $a_1$ with probability $p$; its
worst-case discounted value is
\[
W(p) = \min_{t\in[0,1]} \frac{p(1+t)}{1-\beta p(1-t)}.
\]
At $\beta=0.9$ we have $W(0)=0$, $W(5/9)=10/9$ (constant in $t$), and
$W(5/18)=\min_{t}10(1+t)/(27+9t)=10/27$, attained at $t=0$. Since
$5/18=\tfrac12(0+5/9)$, concavity would require $W(5/18)\ge 5/9$, but
$10/27<5/9$. Hence $W$ is not concave, and the outer maximization is not a
convex program in this regime. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:da-seminorm}}
\label{app:proof-da-seminorm}

\textbf{Seminorm properties.} Nonnegativity is immediate. Positive homogeneity:
$V_v(\mu)$ is linear in $v$, so for $c>0$, $\max_\mu V_{cv}-\min_\mu
V_{cv}=c(\max_\mu V_v-\min_\mu V_v)=c\Dnorm{v}$. Subadditivity: the supremum of
a sum is at most the sum of suprema and the infimum of a sum at least the sum
of infima, so
$\Dnorm{u+v}\le(\max_\mu V_u+\max_\mu V_v)-(\min_\mu V_u+\min_\mu
V_v)=\Dnorm{u}+\Dnorm{v}$.

\textbf{Zero set.} $\Dnorm{v}=0$ iff $V_v(\mu)$ is constant over $M(P)$ iff
$v^\top\Phi^\top(\mu-\nu)=0$ for every $\mu,\nu\in M(P)$, which is the
definition of $v\in\mathcal N$. Every shaping direction $c$ (via potential
$\Phi_{\mathrm{pot}}$) satisfies
$c^\top\Phi^\top\mu=-(1-\beta)\rho_0^\top\Phi_{\mathrm{pot}}$ for every
$\mu\in M(P)$: expand the flow-conservation constraint
$\sum_a\mu(s',a)-\beta\sum_{s,a}P(s,a,s')\mu(s,a)=(1-\beta)\rho_0(s')$ and sum
against $\Phi_{\mathrm{pot}}$. The constant cancels in
$c^\top\Phi^\top(\mu-\nu)$, giving $S_{\mathrm{pot}}\subseteq\mathcal N$. The
containment can be strict: if a state $s$ is unreachable under every policy then
$\mu(s,\cdot)=0$ throughout $M(P)$, so any $v$ whose induced reward is supported
on $s$ lies in $\mathcal N$ without being a potential-shaping term. We do not
claim strictness in general, only that $\mathcal N$ is the exact zero set, which
is what the invariance claims use.

\textbf{Regret bound.} With $\mu^*_\theta$ optimal for $\theta$ and
$\mu_{\theta'}$ optimal for $\theta'$,
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Reg}_\theta(\pi_{\theta'})
&= V_\theta(\mu^*_\theta) - V_\theta(\mu_{\theta'}) \\
&= V_{\theta-\theta'}(\mu^*_\theta) - V_{\theta-\theta'}(\mu_{\theta'})
  + \underbrace{\big[V_{\theta'}(\mu^*_\theta)-V_{\theta'}(\mu_{\theta'})\big]}_{\le\,0
  \text{ since $\mu_{\theta'}$ maximizes }V_{\theta'}} \\
&\le \max_\mu V_{\theta-\theta'}(\mu) - \min_\mu V_{\theta-\theta'}(\mu)
\;=\; \Dnorm{\theta-\theta'},
\end{align*}
the last step because $\mu^*_\theta,\mu_{\theta'}\in M(P)$.

\textbf{Reparametrization invariance.} For invertible $A$, writing
$\tilde\phi=A\phi$ and $\tilde v=A^{-\top}v$, we get
$\tilde\phi(s,a)^\top\tilde v=\phi(s,a)^\top v$ for every $(s,a)$, so the reward
function is unchanged, hence $V_v(\mu)$ at every $\mu\in M(P)$, hence
$\Dnorm{v}$. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:projective}}

On the quotient by $\mathcal N$, $\Dnorm{\cdot}$ is a norm and every nonnull
positive ray has a unit representative, so the triangle inequality, symmetry,
and nonnegativity of $d_\circ$ follow from the seminorm, and
$d_\circ\le\Dnorm{\theta^\circ}+\Dnorm{\theta'^\circ}=2$. Independent positive
rescaling does not change either unit representative; adding an element of
$\mathcal N$ changes a representative only by a seminorm-zero vector; feature
invariance follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:da-seminorm}. If
$d_\circ(\theta,\theta')=0$ then
$\theta/\Dnorm{\theta}-\theta'/\Dnorm{\theta'}\in\mathcal N$, and rearranging
gives $\theta'=c\theta+a$ with $c=\Dnorm{\theta'}/\Dnorm{\theta}>0$ and
$a\in\mathcal N$; the converse is immediate. Finally, optimal policies are
unchanged by positive normalization, so applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:da-seminorm} to the two normalized rewards gives
$\mathrm{Reg}_{\theta}(\pi_{\theta'})/\Dnorm{\theta}
=\mathrm{Reg}_{\theta^\circ}(\pi_{\theta'^\circ})
\le\Dnorm{\theta^\circ-\theta'^\circ}$. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:da-coverage}}
\label{app:proof-da-coverage}

\textbf{Equivalence.} By definition $s_k=\min_{\theta\in K(\pi_k)}
\Dnorm{\theta-\bar\theta^\circ}$, so $s_k\le q$ iff some $\theta\in K(\pi_k)$
achieves $\Dnorm{\theta-\bar\theta^\circ}\le q$, i.e.\ iff $K(\pi_k)\cap
B_q\ne\emptyset$. The epigraph formulation is a feasible, bounded-below LP over
a closed polyhedron, so the optimum is attained; the implementation adds a
large numerical safety cap and rejects a solution if that cap is active.

\textbf{Intersection coverage.} $\bar\theta$ is a fixed measurable function of the training
split, disjoint from the calibration split and the fresh demonstrator.
Conditional on $\bar\theta$, each $s_k$ is a deterministic measurable function
of one i.i.d.\ demonstrator draw, so
$s_1,\dots,s_{N_{\mathrm{val}}},s_{\mathrm{new}}$ are exchangeable. The
ascending rank of $s_{\mathrm{new}}$ among these $N_{\mathrm{val}}+1$ reals is
uniform on $\{1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{val}}+1\}$, so
$\mathbb P[\mathrm{rank}(s_{\mathrm{new}})\le\tau]=\tau/(N_{\mathrm{val}}+1)\ge
\gamma$ by the choice of $\tau$. With ties broken uniformly at random,
$\mathrm{rank}(s_{\mathrm{new}})\le\tau$ implies
$s_{\mathrm{new}}\le q_\gamma$; the converse need not hold with ties, so the
nonrandomized threshold is conservative. The sentinel case covers every score.
$\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}}
\label{app:proof-containment}

On the Stage-I event, choose a witness $\tilde\theta\in K(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})$
with $\Dnorm{\tilde\theta-\bar\theta^\circ}\le q_\gamma$. If $q_\gamma<1$, the
reverse triangle inequality gives $\Dnorm{\tilde\theta}\ge1-q_\gamma>0$, and
since $K(\pi)$ is a cone, $\tilde\theta^\circ$ belongs to
$F^\circ(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})$. Moreover
\[
\Dnorm{\tilde\theta^\circ-\bar\theta^\circ}
\le \Dnorm{\tilde\theta^\circ-\tilde\theta}+\Dnorm{\tilde\theta-\bar\theta^\circ}
\le|1-\Dnorm{\tilde\theta}|+q_\gamma\le2q_\gamma.
\]
The latent normalized reward lies in the same cone, so
Assumption~\ref{ass:cone-width} and the triangle inequality give
$\Dnorm{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}^\circ-\bar\theta^\circ}\le2q_\gamma+\eta$;
when $q_\gamma\ge1$ this is subsumed by the cap $R=2$ from the universal
projective diameter. Proposition~\ref{prop:da-coverage} assigns probability at
least $\gamma$ to the Stage-I event.

For the robust statement, $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is feasible and has relative
value zero under every reward, so the optimum at $\mu_R$ is nonnegative. On the
containment event $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}^\circ\in B_R$, hence its relative
value at $\mu_R$ is at least the worst-case relative value and therefore at
least zero; positive rescaling back to $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{new}}$ preserves the
inequality. Subtracting both values from the same optimal value gives the
equivalent regret statement. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:degeneracy}}

Let $x=\Phi^\top(\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})/(1-\beta)$. Polar duality and
$\Dnorm{\bar\theta^\circ}=1$ give
$V_{\bar\theta^\circ}(\mu)-V_{\bar\theta^\circ}(\mu_{\mathrm{ref}})
\le\gamma_{D_F}(x)$, so the relative robust objective is at most
$(1-R)\gamma_{D_F}(x)\le0$ when $R\ge1$. The reference has $x=0$ and attains
zero. If $R>1$, equality requires $\gamma_{D_F}(x)=0$, i.e.\ the two occupancies
have the same feature vector modulo the null directions; at $R=1$ additional
exposed-face ties can attain zero. $\blacksquare$
